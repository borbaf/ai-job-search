## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why did you leave Vibra after 13 years?"
> Thirteen years, five roles, ending as Superintendent of the Tubarão terminal — I got everything out of that company I could reasonably ask for. What I wanted next was breadth: seeing how other operations solve the same problems. That's what took me to consulting at Moby, where I worked across forestry, food, fuels and agribusiness in two years. No negativity — it remains the place that taught me the industry.

### "Why did you leave Moby / what are you doing now?"
> The consulting work was where I first applied Python, SQL and Power BI to real client problems, and it convinced me that analytics was the direction, not a side skill. Since mid-2025 I've been consulting through GLG's expert network while completing the Google Cloud Data Analytics certification and a postgraduate degree in Data Science and Big Data. It's a deliberate bridge, and I'm looking for the full-time role it bridges to.

### "You don't have 5 years as a Data Analyst."
> Correct — I have two years of applied analytics inside supply chain consulting, and 15 years running the operations that supply chain analytics is about. Most analysts in this space have to be taught what OTIF costs, why a terminal's receipt window matters, or what makes a freight number wrong. I've owned those decisions with a 50-person organization and a state's fuel supply behind them. The tooling I've built deliberately: Python, SQL, BigQuery, Power BI, certified and applied on client work. The domain knowledge is the part that takes years, and I already have it.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Leading analytics for a supply chain or operations organization — the person who owns both the data and the operational judgment about what it means. I've led large teams before, so the management path isn't new to me; what's new is doing it from the data side. Short term, I want two or three years of depth as an individual contributor delivering real analytical work before taking a team again.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> Formal data engineering. I can build the pipeline that gets an analysis done — Python, SQL, APIs, scraping — but I haven't run orchestration or a warehouse at production scale, and I won't pretend otherwise. I'm closing it deliberately: the Google Cloud certification covered cloud storage and transformation with BigQuery, and the postgraduate program is on big data architecture. In the meantime I'm explicit with teams about where my line is, which I'd argue is safer than a candidate who overstates it.

### "Your English — how comfortable are you working in it daily?"
> Advanced. I worked a full engineering role in Dublin in English — commissioning, client-facing support, and prospecting with the sales team — and my GLG consultations are in English. I'm not going to claim native fluency; I will claim I've already done the job in it. <!-- Only use if asked. Do not volunteer a caveat. -->

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
